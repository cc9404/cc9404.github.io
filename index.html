Feature 01 · Marketing analytics

Telco Customer Retention & Churn Analytics

Goal: find which customers churn, when they churn, and where a retention plan should step in.

  • Analyzed 7,043 telecom accounts with SQL.
  • Surfaced a 55.2% churn cliff in the first six months.
  • Showed that bundle strategies can push churn below 15%.
  • Built on the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset with MySQL 8.0, using multi-tier CTEs, NTILE and DENSE_RANK window functions, and revenue cohort modeling.
  • Timing: month-to-month contracts lose 55.20% of customers in the first 180 days, leaking $49,681.30 in monthly recurring revenue.
  • Triage: 713 high-value subscribers sit in the highest-risk tercile, carrying $50,848.85 in monthly revenue at risk. These accounts justify high-touch management.
  • Causes: competitor offers (33.23%) and support friction (31.40%) account for over 64% of churn.
  • Defense: single-play internet users churn at 52.24%. Subscribers with four or more add-ons fall under 15%, and full six-feature users reach 5.28%, while ARPU rises from $58.59 to $99.37.

Feature 04 · Marketing analytics

Top 100 US Independent Restaurants Clustering

Goal: segment the highest-grossing independent US restaurants by how they earn, not just how much.

  • K-Means clustering on average check size and annual sales.
  • Four distinct business models, with the number of clusters chosen by the elbow method.
  • Ranking by sales alone hides the business model: a $20M restaurant can serve many guests at $35, or a few at $150.
  • The elbow method showed a clear turning point at K = 4, where adding clusters stopped reducing error meaningfully.
  • Mega-volume outliers: about $40 to $45 per check and $35M+ in sales, built on guest throughput and table turnover.
  • High-grossing premium venues: about $85 to $90 per check and $22M to $28M in sales, with strong pricing power.
  • Steady high performers: about $75 to $80 per check and $17M to $20M in sales, with steady, profitable demand.
  • Core market challengers: about $50 to $60 per check and $11M to $15M in sales, relying on consistent neighborhood traffic.

Feature 05 · Marketing analytics

Market Basket Analysis

Goal: find which products customers buy together, and use those pairings to design bundles and cross-sell offers.

  • Apriori association rules on 380 French transaction baskets covering 1,541 products.
  • Filtered for lift of at least 2.0 and confidence of at least 60%.
  • Recommended partyware bundles, tiered collector pricing, and checkout cross-sells.
  • Cleaned 541K+ raw transactions down to 380 French baskets covering 1,541 products, removing cancellations, postage fees, and non-merchandise codes.
  • Mined association rules with the Apriori algorithm, keeping only pairs with lift of at least 2.0 and confidence of at least 60%.
  • The network graph separates the catalog into communities: partyware (paper cups, plates, napkins), retro alarm clocks, children's cutlery, and skull-themed tableware.
  • The lift heatmap shows strong co-purchase inside each community, such as lift of 12.57 for children's cutlery and 13.82 for skull tableware. Alarm clocks bought across colors still show lift above 7.2, which points to collectors building a set rather than swapping one color for another.
  • Recommendations: a "Picnic Essentials Kit" with a 10% to 12% bundle discount, tiered pricing on alarm clocks ("Buy 2, Get 15% Off"), a checkout cross-sell for cutlery, and co-locating associated products in adjacent warehouse aisles.

Team project · Competitive strategy

RX Bar Growth Strategy

Goal: defend RXBAR in a category where clean-label is no longer enough to win, and find the next growth segments.

  • Protect, Extend, and Explore: defend the core, reach women's health and children's nutrition, then lead the functional snacking shift.
  • Three growth segments for the next three to five years, each with its own way to win.
  • A five-year roadmap that moves RXBAR from a clean-label snack brand into a broader functional wellness platform.
  • Team project with Shuran Cheng, Ethan Chou, Natthaya Kangsanarak, Ariella Li, and Hazel Liu.
  • Where RXBAR competes: the protein bar and better-for-you snack bar categories. Core consumers are fitness-oriented people and clean-label seekers. The brand is sold through Whole Foods, Target, Costco, gyms, and e-commerce.
  • The problem: transparency is now table stakes. KIND and Clif Bar have improved ingredient messaging and reduced sugar, so clean-label no longer sets RXBAR apart. Big brands compete on scale and taste, and small brands compete on specialization and function.
  • Protect: defend the core active-consumer and clean-label base.
  • Extend: reach women's health (iron, collagen, hormonal health) and children's nutrition, where RX Kids is already launched and parents want clean-label, low-sugar snacks.
  • Explore: lead the functional snacking shift with new product lines that use real ingredients, such as adaptogens and nootropics, on the clean-label platform.
  • Winning position: other brands are indulgent (taste-first), performance (function-first), or functional but complex. RXBAR owns the space between simple and functional.
  • Roadmap: Year 1 to 2 protect and build, Year 2 to 3 enter and innovate, Year 3 to 5 scale and acquire, with 1 to 2 targeted acquisitions in functional snacking, women's health, and children's nutrition.
  • Capabilities to win: product innovation, consumer insights and personalization, digital and CRM, omnichannel distribution, and brand education.

MUD JEANS Client Permanent Campaign Plan

Goal: design a permanent campaign that makes leasing and repairing MUD JEANS feel easy and desirable, and prove the impact with measurable marketing efficiency.

  • Presented to the client on 29 April 2026 by a six-person team.
  • My role: the ROMI framework, turning insights into impact with measurable marketing efficiency.
  • MUD replaces the traditional buy, wear, discard model with a wear, return, repair, reuse system. Each pair is built for durability and multiple life cycles, with free repairs and leasing.
  • Four key problems: the adoption barrier of circular models, a premium price that is hard to justify, friction in conversion and the customer experience, and scaling circularity in a crowded market.
  • The impact formula combines resonance (great design, comfort, and fit) with alignment (running circularity as a service, with proof) to create stakeholder value, social impact, and business advantage through trust and lifetime value.
  • The core insight: the barrier is psychology rather than sustainability. Consumers support circularity, but leasing feels unfamiliar and complex. The opportunity is to simplify and normalize leasing as an easy, desirable entry point.
  • The ROMI framework connects these insights to measurable marketing efficiency, showing what we did and how it creates impact.
